# NASA data and resources used

Every source below is also listed in the app on the Sources page, together with where it is used.

## NASA Human Research Program, 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight
https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/

- **Overview, "Spaceflight hazards" panel:** one plain-English line for each of the five hazards, and the footnote "The hazards can feed off one another and worsen effects on the body."
- **Body Map, "Hazards that affect it":** each body system lists the hazards that affect it, worded from this page.
- **Overview, "Hazard priority in this environment":** the one-line reasons draw on this page, for example "Mars gravity is three-eighths of Earth's". The ranking itself is a team decision and is labelled as one.

## NASA Human Research Program, Risks of Human Spaceflight
https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/

- **Body Map:** all eight body systems link to named risks, for example Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) for the eyes, and Altered Immune System Responses and Radiation-Induced Cancers for the immune system and blood. Each risk links to this page.
- **Triage protocols:** the sleep, mood, vision, exercise, SpO2 and radiation protocols link the related risks.
- **Vitals:** the exercise adherence and vision self-check notes cite the related risks.
- **Sources page, About VIGIL:** VIGIL addresses the named Risk of Inadequate Systems Operations Support.

## NASA Human Research Program, Ongoing HRP Research
https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/

- **Routine, "NASA research in progress" card, and Body Map, "Related NASA research":** ARCHeR, B-Complex, Thigh Cuff, Zero T2, Space Gardening and Tempus Pro, each with a one-line description. Countermeasures still under investigation are labelled "NASA research in progress" and are never shown as proven treatments.

## NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1
- **[V1 4030], career radiation limit of 600 mSv:** the radiation panel gauge and days remaining, the radiation protocol and the Downlink brief.
- **[V1 4031], solar particle event limit of 250 mSv per event:** the radiation panel, the solar particle event alert and the radiation protocol.

## NASA Mars Science Laboratory (Curiosity) Radiation Assessment Detector
- **Cruise dose rate of 1.84 mSv/day (Zeitlin et al., 2013, Science):** the Spacecraft Transit rate in the radiation panel, the simulated mission dose (30 days in transit), the Body Map environment notes and the hazard priority reasons.
- **Mars surface dose rate of 0.64 mSv/day (Hassler et al., 2014, Science):** the Pressurized Rover and Surface Base rates in the same places.

## NASA ISS crew dosimetry
- **Orbital Station dose rate of 0.2 to 0.5 mSv/day (0.35 used for calculations):** the radiation panel and Body Map.

## NASA Mars Relay Network
- **One-way light time of about 3 to 22 minutes:** the top bar's signal delay indicator, the comm-delay escalation rule (Earth reply time is twice the one-way delay), the Downlink arrival time and the hazard priority reasons.

## NASA Cognition test battery (Basner et al., 2015)
- **Vitals and Overview:** the context note on the cognitive readiness score.
