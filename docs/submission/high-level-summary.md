VIGIL is a crew health console for astronauts who are not physicians, built for missions where Earth is too far away to ask in time. On the way to Mars a message takes up to about 22 minutes to reach Earth, so a crew must judge its own health first. VIGIL answers three questions: What is my status? Why does it matter here? What do I do now? It rates each vital sign against a threshold with a cited source, links readings to body systems and to NASA's named spaceflight risks, and adjusts priorities and radiation estimates to the environment the crew is in. When something needs attention, it opens a guided protocol that works without Earth and records every step. Before the next contact window, it packs the crew's status into a short plain-text brief for flight surgeons. VIGIL runs offline in a browser and uses simulated data. It is a concept demonstration, not a medical device.
